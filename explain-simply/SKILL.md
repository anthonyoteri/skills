---

You explain one technical concept so its audience understands it on one read, using the CLEAR filter from Vinh Giang's [How to Explain Anything To Anyone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sqh6rt1hN2A). Jargon and acronyms make you sound smart and leave the audience behind, so every word you use is one they already know.

## 1. Calibrate

Needs no CLIs or other skills.

Settle the **audience** and what they already know about the concept. A named audience or level in the request ("for my manager", "I know Python but not Kubernetes") settles it. Otherwise ask one question, "How familiar are you with `<concept>`?", and wait for the answer. State the audience you settled on in one line. For specialists, skip the rest and explain at their depth in their terms.

Done when you can name what the audience knows and what they lack.

## 2. Link

Pick one analogy, metaphor or simile from something the audience already knows, and map the concept onto it part by part. Everyday objects and shared experience beat another technical domain.

Done when every part of the concept the explanation needs has a counterpart in the link.

## 3. Envision

When words alone are hard to hold, draw the concept: a box-and-arrow flow, a triangle, a circle, a short staged model, in a plain-text diagram. A concept that one sentence carries needs no drawing.

Done when the diagram shows the whole idea and every label is a word from the audience's vocabulary.

## 4. Abstract

Boil the idea down to its simplest form, in the words you would use with a six-year-old: short sentences, one idea each, every acronym spelled out or replaced.

Done when no term remains that the audience didn't already have in step 1 or that the link and diagram didn't introduce.

## 5. Repeat

Close the loop: end with one sentence that returns to the shape of the idea, the link's image in the audience's words.

## Reply

Reply with the explanation itself: the link, the diagram if any, the plain-language walkthrough, then the closing sentence. Leave the five step names out.
